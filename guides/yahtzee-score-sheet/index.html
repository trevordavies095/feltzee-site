The Yahtzee Score Sheet, Explained

A classic five-dice score sheet has 13 boxes: six upper-section boxes (Aces through Sixes) that score matching dice, and seven lower-section boxes: 3 of a Kind, 4 of a Kind, Full House (25), Small Straight (30), Large Straight (40), 5 of a Kind (50), and Chance.

The upper section

The upper section is the counting half of the sheet. Each box scores only the dice matching its number:

BoxWhat it scoresExample
AcesTotal of all 1s1-1-3-4-6 → 2
TwosTotal of all 2s2-2-2-5-6 → 6
ThreesTotal of all 3s3-3-1-4-5 → 6
FoursTotal of all 4s4-4-4-4-2 → 16
FivesTotal of all 5s5-5-5-1-2 → 15
SixesTotal of all 6s6-6-6-6-6 → 30

If your six upper boxes total 63 or more, you earn a 35-point bonus. Sixty-three is exactly three of each number, which is why experienced players guard their Fives and Sixes.

The lower section

BoxRequirementPoints
3 of a KindThree matching diceTotal of all 5 dice
4 of a KindFour matching diceTotal of all 5 dice
Full HouseThree of one number + two of another25
Small StraightRun of four (e.g. 2-3-4-5)30
Large StraightRun of five (1-2-3-4-5 or 2-3-4-5-6)40
5 of a KindAll five dice matching50
ChanceAny diceTotal of all 5 dice

Below the boxes, a full sheet also tracks the 5-of-a-kind bonus (100 points for each additional five of a kind after your first 50) and the grand total.

Quick answers

What is the bonus box on the score sheet?

There are two: the upper bonus (35 points for reaching 63+ in the upper section) and the 5-of-a-kind bonus (100 points per additional five of a kind after the first, scored at 50).

Can each box be used more than once?

No. Every box is filled exactly once, which is why a game lasts exactly 13 turns.
