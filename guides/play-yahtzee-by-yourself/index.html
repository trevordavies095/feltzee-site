Can You Play Yahtzee by Yourself? Yes, Here's How

Yes. The classic five-dice game works perfectly solo. Play the standard 13 turns and try to beat your own best score. Nothing about the rules requires an opponent; the scorecard itself is the challenge.

How solo play works

The rules don't change: 13 turns, up to three rolls each, every box filled once. What changes is the goal: instead of beating an opponent, you're playing against the scorecard and your own history. Solo players tend to obsess over exactly the right things: the upper bonus, smart zeros, and long-shot five-of-a-kind chases, because every decision is yours alone.

Making solo play competitive

  • Keep a high-score list. A running top 10 turns isolated games into a career. Beating a personal best from three weeks ago is a real win.
  • Set threshold goals. Crack 250, then 300, then 350. See what counts as a good score for honest benchmarks.
  • Compete asynchronously. Daily leaderboards let you play alone but rank against everyone who played today.

Why Feltzee is built for solo play

Feltzee is a solo player's home table. Games save automatically mid-turn, so a round fits into any spare moment: play two turns in a queue, finish on the couch. Finished scores save by name to a local top 10, which doubles as your personal record book. Haptics-only feedback means you can grind for a new personal best in bed, in a meeting-adjacent situation, anywhere quiet.

Quick answers

Is solo play different from multiplayer rules?

No. The turns, boxes, and bonuses are identical. Only the win condition changes: your past best instead of another player.

What's a good solo goal to start with?

Beat 200 consistently, then chase 250. Once the upper bonus becomes routine, 300 comes into reach.
